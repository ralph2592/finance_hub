## What the two plans are
Every mutual fund scheme can be held through a direct plan or a regular plan. In a direct plan, the investor invests without routing the investment through a distributor or agent. In a regular plan, the investment is made with the help of a distributor or agent. This difference in investment route is the key distinction between the two plans. 

## Why the costs differ
A direct plan and a regular plan have different expense ratios, because a direct plan has no distribution cost or commission. SEBI has noted that distribution expenses and commission cannot be charged to investors of a direct plan, so the direct plan has a lower expense ratio and the two plans show different returns. This difference in expense ratios is a result of the absence of distribution costs in direct plans.

## What stays the same
A direct plan and a regular plan belong to the same scheme, share the same portfolio, and are managed by the same fund manager. This means that the investment strategy and the underlying assets remain the same for both plans. The only difference lies in the distribution channel and the associated costs.

## Separate NAVs and what that means
Because the expense ratios differ, each plan has its own NAV, and the direct plan has the higher NAV. AMFI notes that the difference between the two NAVs tends to be small. However, over time, the lower cost of a direct plan can add to returns, and this difference keeps compounding. This means that even a small difference in NAV can result in significant differences in returns over the long term.

## How to tell which plan you hold
Schemes show direct or regular in the plan name, and SEBI requires expenses and returns to be disclosed separately for direct and regular plans. Regular plan investments are made under the distributor's AMFI registration number, known as an ARN. Banks commonly act as distributors, so investing through a bank is generally a regular plan and not a direct plan. Investors can check their investment documents or consult with their distributor to determine which plan they hold.

## Switching between plans
Moving from a regular plan to a direct plan can involve an exit load and tax on gains, so it is crucial to check the scheme documents and the current tax rules first. Additionally, the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026 changed how expense limits are defined from 1 April 2026, so investors should check the current expense ratio in the scheme documents before making any changes. AMFI describes direct plans as meant for investors who prefer to invest without a distributor's help, and regular plans as the route for investors who want guidance and assistance. Investors should carefully consider their needs and goals before deciding which plan to choose or switch to.
