## What a factsheet is
A factsheet is a short monthly document published by a fund house that summarises a scheme. SEBI requires fund houses to publish factsheets every month. This document provides a concise overview of the scheme, making it easier for investors to understand the scheme's details.

## Where to find it
Factsheets are available on fund house websites and on AMFI's website. Investors can access these documents to gather information about a particular scheme. By checking the factsheet, investors can get a sense of the scheme's performance, portfolio, and other relevant details.

## The scheme basics
A factsheet typically shows the scheme's category and investment objective, its NAV, its assets under management and details of the fund manager. The scheme's category and investment objective provide insight into the scheme's goals and the types of investments it makes. The NAV and assets under management give an idea of the scheme's size and performance. The details of the fund manager, including how long they have managed the scheme, are also important, as a change of fund manager can change how a scheme is run.

## Portfolio and holdings
The portfolio section shows the largest holdings and the split across asset types and sectors. This information helps investors understand the scheme's investment strategy and the types of assets it holds. By examining the portfolio, investors can gain insight into the scheme's diversification and potential risks.

## Performance and benchmark
Past performance is shown over different periods and compared with the scheme's benchmark index. The benchmark is the index against which the scheme's returns are compared, and it should suit the category of the scheme. Past performance does not guarantee future results, but it can provide a sense of the scheme's historical performance. Investors should consider the benchmark when evaluating the scheme's performance, as it provides a basis for comparison.

## Costs and risk
The expense ratio is shown as a yearly percentage of assets, and the exit load is listed. The portfolio turnover ratio shows how actively the fund trades, and frequent trading adds costs inside the fund. Risk measures such as standard deviation, beta and the Sharpe ratio may be shown. The riskometer is a SEBI-mandated colour-coded indicator of a scheme's risk, with six levels from Low to Very High. Investors should consider these costs and risk measures when evaluating a scheme, as they can impact the scheme's performance and suitability for their investment goals. It is also important to note that the factsheet is a summary, and the scheme information document has the full terms. Additionally, the factsheet shows the latest month, so an older factsheet will not show recent changes, and investors should check the current figure in the scheme or policy document for the most up-to-date information.
