## What a SIP is
A systematic investment plan (SIP) is a method of investing a fixed amount in a mutual fund scheme at regular intervals, such as monthly. It is not a separate product, but rather a way of investing in an existing mutual fund scheme. The investor chooses the scheme, the amount, the date and the frequency, and sets up an auto-debit instruction (mandate) with their bank.

## How a SIP works step by step
The SIP process works as follows: on each specified date, the fixed amount is debited from the investor's bank account and used to buy units of the chosen mutual fund scheme at that day's net asset value (NAV). As a result, more units are allotted when the NAV is low and fewer when it is high. This process allows investors to invest a fixed amount of money at regular intervals, regardless of the market's performance.

## Rupee cost averaging: what it does and does not do
The averaging of purchase cost that occurs with a SIP is called rupee cost averaging. This concept reduces dependence on picking a single entry point, as investments are spread across several dates. However, it does not protect against losses or guarantee returns, as mutual fund returns are not guaranteed and the NAV can fall as well as rise.

## SIP versus lump sum investing
A SIP differs from a lump sum investment, which buys units at a single NAV on one date. In contrast, a SIP spreads purchases across several dates, allowing investors to invest a fixed amount of money at regular intervals. This can help reduce the impact of market volatility, as investments are made at different points in time.

## Costs, lock-ins and rules to check
Before investing in a SIP, it is crucial to check the scheme documents for minimum SIP amounts, available frequencies, and any applicable lock-ins or exit loads. For example, most open-ended funds have no lock-in, but ELSS tax-saving funds carry a 3-year lock-in that applies separately to each SIP instalment. Additionally, some schemes charge an exit load if units are redeemed within a period set by the scheme.

## What happens if an instalment fails
If a SIP instalment cannot be debited because the bank account has insufficient balance, that instalment is not invested, and the bank may charge a fee for the failed debit. Fund houses may cancel a SIP after repeated failed debits, according to their own rules. If a SIP date falls on a holiday, the instalment is typically processed on the next business day.
