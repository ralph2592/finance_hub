## What a term plan is
A pure term plan pays the sum assured to the nominee if the policyholder dies during the policy term; it pays nothing if the policyholder survives the term. This type of plan provides life cover for a specified period, making it a basic form of life insurance. 

## What an endowment plan is
An endowment plan combines life cover with savings: it pays a death benefit during the term and a maturity benefit if the policyholder survives. This means that part of the premium paid goes towards providing a life cover, while the remaining amount is invested to provide a savings component. 

## The key differences
Because part of an endowment premium goes to the savings component, premiums are generally higher than for a pure term plan with the same life cover. Some term plans offer a return of premium option, which raises the premium. This difference in premium is due to the savings component in endowment plans, which is not present in term plans. 

## Premiums and what they depend on
A term plan premium depends on factors such as the policyholder age, the sum assured, the policy term and the insurer assessment of health and lifestyle. The policy term and the premium payment term can differ in some plans, so check both in the policy document. 

## Benefits, bonuses and early exit
In traditional endowment plans, only part of the maturity amount may be guaranteed; bonuses, where offered, are not guaranteed. Surrendering an endowment plan before maturity can return less than the premiums paid, depending on the surrender terms in the policy. Riders such as accidental death or critical illness cover can be added to some plans for an additional premium, and the available riders differ by insurer and plan. 

## What to compare before buying
Compare the sum assured, policy term, premium payment term, exclusions, and the insurer's claim settlement record. Exclusions and conditions differ by insurer and are listed in the policy document. The claim settlement ratio is a published figure showing the share of claims an insurer settled in a year, and it does not predict how any individual claim will be treated. 

## Disclosure, nominees and claims
Health and lifestyle details must be disclosed accurately at purchase, since non-disclosure can lead to a claim being rejected. The policyholder names a nominee to receive the benefit, and nominee details can be updated as circumstances change. Insurers in India are regulated by the IRDAI. Tax treatment of premiums and benefits depends on the rules in force at the time, which change, so check the current rules. A free-look period lets a policyholder review a new policy and return it if the terms are not acceptable, subject to the insurer rules.
