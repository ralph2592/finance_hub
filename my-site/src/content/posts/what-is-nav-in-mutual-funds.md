## What NAV means
NAV stands for Net Asset Value and is the per-unit value of a mutual fund scheme. This value represents the market value of the scheme's assets minus its liabilities, divided by the number of units outstanding. NAV is a key concept in mutual fund investing, as it provides a benchmark for the value of each unit of the scheme.

## How NAV is calculated
NAV is calculated as the market value of the scheme's assets minus its liabilities, divided by the number of units outstanding. Assets are valued at the closing market prices of the securities the scheme holds, so NAV changes from day to day as those prices change. This means that the NAV of a mutual fund scheme can fluctuate constantly, reflecting the changes in the value of the underlying assets. The calculation of NAV is a critical process, as it provides investors with a clear picture of the scheme's value.

## When NAV is published
Unlike a share price, which moves during market hours, a scheme's NAV is declared at the end of each trading day after markets close. Fund houses publish NAVs every business day on their own websites and on AMFI's website. The publication of NAV is a regular process, and investors can rely on it to stay up-to-date with the scheme's performance.

## Which NAV applies to your purchase
For most schemes, units are allotted at the prospective NAV, which is the NAV declared at the end of the day based on closing prices. However, liquid and overnight funds follow different rules. The NAV that applies to a purchase depends on the cut-off time and on when the money is available. An application received after the cut-off time gets the NAV of the next business day. Cut-off times are set out in the scheme documents, and investors should be aware of these times to ensure that they understand which NAV applies to their purchase.

## What NAV does and does not tell you
A fund with a NAV of ₹10 and a fund with a NAV of ₹50 that each rise by 10% give the same percentage gain, so a lower NAV does not mean a fund is cheaper. The number of units allotted is the amount invested divided by the NAV. NAV does not show how good a fund is. What matters is how the NAV changes over time and what the fund costs. Scheme expenses are deducted from the scheme's assets, so they are reflected in the daily NAV and not billed separately. Direct and regular plans of the same scheme have separate NAVs. Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks, so it is crucial to read all scheme-related documents carefully to understand the risks and costs involved.
