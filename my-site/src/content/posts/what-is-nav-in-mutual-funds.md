## What NAV means
NAV stands for Net Asset Value and is the per-unit value of a mutual fund scheme. It represents the value of each unit of the scheme. NAV is a key concept in mutual fund investing, and understanding what it means is crucial for investors.

## How NAV is calculated
NAV is calculated as the market value of the scheme's assets minus its liabilities, divided by the number of units outstanding. Assets are valued at the closing market prices of the securities the scheme holds. This means that NAV changes from day to day as the prices of the securities in the scheme's portfolio change.

## A worked example
For example, a scheme with assets worth ₹110 crore, liabilities of ₹10 crore and 10 crore units outstanding has a NAV of ₹10. This is because ₹110 crore minus ₹10 crore is ₹100 crore, and ₹100 crore divided by 10 crore units is ₹10. If an investor invests ₹10,000 at a NAV of ₹20, they will buy 500 units. Later, if the NAV rises from ₹20 to ₹22, the 500 units will be worth ₹11,000.

## When NAV is published
Unlike a share price, which moves during market hours, a scheme's NAV is declared at the end of each trading day after markets close. Fund houses publish NAVs every business day on their own websites and on AMFI's website. This provides investors with a clear picture of the scheme's value at the end of each trading day.

## Which NAV applies to your purchase
For most schemes, units are allotted at the prospective NAV, which is the NAV declared at the end of the day based on closing prices. However, which day NAV applies depends on the cut-off time and on when the money is available. An application received after the cut-off time gets the NAV of the next business day. Cut-off times are set out in the scheme documents, and investors should be aware of these times when investing in a scheme.

## What NAV does and does not tell you
NAV does not show how good a fund is. What matters is how the NAV changes over time and what the fund costs. A fund with a NAV of ₹10 and a fund with a NAV of ₹50 that each rise by 10% give the same percentage gain, so a lower NAV does not mean a fund is cheaper. The NAV is quoted per unit, so it does not show the size of a scheme. Assets under management show that. Scheme expenses are deducted from the scheme's assets, so they are reflected in the daily NAV and not billed separately. Direct and regular plans of the same scheme have separate NAVs. Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks, and investors should read all scheme-related documents carefully before investing. On redemption, the amount paid is the number of units multiplied by the applicable NAV, less any exit load.
